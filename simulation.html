Simulations

Interactive physics-based models of confluent biological tissue. Each simulation runs directly in the browser — adjust parameters in real time and observe how the tissue responds.

Model 01 · Quasi-Static · Simple Shear

2D Voronoi Tissue Under Simple Shear

A quasi-static (AQS) model of a confluent monolayer deformed in simple shear. Cell shapes emerge from the Voronoi tessellation of N = 100 particle centers. Each cell resists area changes, capturing cell incompressibility, and has a preferred perimeter set by the competition between cortical contractility and line tension at junctions. The ratio of the preferred perimeter to the square root of the preferred area, p₀, is the key parameter that control tissue rigidity. At each strain step of δγ = 0.001, particle positions are sheared affinely and relaxed to a local energy minimum. Yielding is purely mechanical — there is no thermal noise — and occurs through system-spanning cascades of T1 neighbor exchanges.

What you can study

  • Yield stress and the jamming transition
  • T1 avalanche statistics
  • Mechanical memory and oscillatory training
  • Stress–strain curves and hysteresis
  • Fatigue under cyclic loading

Model 02 · Active Vertex Model · Dual-Role Adhesion

Vertex Model with Dual-Role Cell–Cell Adhesion

A 2D active vertex model of N = 100 polygonal cells whose edges meet at trivalent vertices. Each cell resists area changes, capturing incompressibility, and has a preferred perimeter governed by the balance of cortical contractility and line tension; the ratio of target perimeter to cell size, p₀, controls where the tissue sits relative to the rigidity transition. Cell–cell adhesion enters through two independent channels: energetic adhesion (p₀) lowers the effective line tension at shared junctions; viscous adhesion (B) introduces frictional resistance to differential sliding at junctions, slowing relative motion.

What you can study

  • Dual role of adhesion in tissue fluidity
  • Jamming and unjamming transitions
  • Viscous dissipation and effective tissue viscosity
  • Collective cell migration

Model 03 · Active Brownian Particles · Homeostasis

Active Cells with Voronoi Geometry and Homeostasis

N₀ = 900 active Brownian particles evolve in a 30σ × 30σ periodic domain. Each particle self-propels at speed v₀ along a slowly rotating orientation with rotational diffusion Dr, and repels neighbors through a soft contact force. Cell shapes emerge from the Voronoi tessellation of particle centers — no energy function governs cell geometry; shape is a purely geometric consequence of packing. Homeostasis is enforced stochastically: a cell divides along its principal inertia axis when its area becomes sufficiently large, and undergoes apoptosis when it becomes too small, with rates k_div and k_apo set by the user.

What you can study

  • Cell area distributions and their statistical form
  • Effect of motility, cell division and apoptosis on spatial order and heterogeneity
